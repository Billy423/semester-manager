# Ring 0's row carries seven fields at its obligation position and four at its progress position, derived at #86 from the judgments; `parts` and `grade_share` are excluded, and `grade_share`'s exclusion rests on a corpus argument

Ring 0's row carries `id`, `name`, `due`, `done_by`, `optional`, `course` and `has-more` at its obligation position, and `id`, `state`, `origin` and `updated_at` at the progress position its standing intent reaches (`0103`). `parts` is out because it answers *what is this about* rather than *where do I look next*; `grade_share` is out because a rendered column of shares reads as a partition of the grade that it is not, which is the single largest measured faithfulness defect in the corpus. **Excluded from the projection is not unreadable**: ring 0 governs residency, and `parts` comes back with any read of the obligation record.

```
obligation position : id · name · due · done_by · optional · course · has-more
progress position   : id · state · origin · updated_at
neither             : parts · grade_share · grade_share_conditional · added_at
```

**This record's field set was first chosen by applying ring 0's purpose as a test over `obligation`'s existing field table, which `0100` diagnosed as construction bottom-up. At #86 it was derived top-down** - from the judgments the coordinator must make, under `0101`'s criterion, at every position of the standing intent - and the derivation lands on `0038`'s seven with `state` re-typed as a position and the handle made explicit - seven at the obligation position, four at the progress position (`0103`). That is an independent confirmation of this selection on a different ground, and the ground is now `0103`'s. The two bands this record once fixed, band A with seven fields and band B with four, are dissolved: a result carries one field set per kind, and the three triggers that partitioned the rows are the coordinator's judgment rule (`0042`).

**The exclusions carry arguments of their own.** `grade_share`'s was audited against the 2c03 corpus at #82 and holds; `0103` records that `0101`'s criterion alone does not close the field for the *what to do next* judgment and that this corpus argument is what closes it. An argument that stands is not thereby a derivation, and the audit is not reproducible from this checkout.

**The only sizing number that matters is that ring 0 for five courses is roughly 55 obligations.** **Its evidence base is two courses that look alike:** 2c03 and 2aa4 are both topic-dense and obligation-sparse, and 2px3 - the obligation-dense shape - was excluded throughout the material this figure comes from. The one course left out is the one whose shape would move the number. Holding every obligation resident is a bet on this figure and on one term, recorded at `0103`, and what would wake it is a measurement that includes the dense course or a second term landing.

**The measurement carried inline because its source does not migrate, and what it actually measures.** The figures below were attributed here to the share-as-partition defect and **they are not that defect's**: at source they are rank 1 *"the conditional weighting printed as a fixed number"* plus rank 6 *"a floor restated as a point value"*, which the source itself glosses as one defect - *"the schema holds a scalar where the course states a conditional or a bound"*. That is ADR `0032`'s subject. **The exclusion's surviving ground is the independent corpus argument below, not these numbers.** The figures, for the record: the conditional-flattened-to-a-scalar defect is 24 claims across 17 runs; with a floor restated as a point value it is 29 of 77, **38% of every measured faithfulness failure**, and the only defect kind appearing in every configuration group. All 60 runs were graded by an independent grader given the run's own tool output and its answer and nothing else: over 870 run-item pairs, **77 unsupported · 32 contradicted · 449 well-handled**. **Standing: these numbers have not been re-derived structurally, which the source's own warranty asks for before any of its metrics is trusted.** **And the grader was found wrong where it could be checked:** it invented a claim that `look_at` *"isn't among the system's tools"* when it had never been shown a tool list, so its own source calls the rank-3 counts *"unreliable in both directions"*; a second instrument in the same directory disagrees on 20 of the 60 runs and was right in all three hand-checked cases. Rank 3 - claims about tool use, 14 of the 77 - is *"inflated by construction"*, because the grader never saw the call log. An independent corpus argument survives without them - one course's share column sums to 95, the missing 5% has no row, and two 1% bonuses sit outside the 100.
