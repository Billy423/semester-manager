# Corpus measurements bound what the material contains, never what a record should look like

Measurements over the extracted corpus are admissible for what the source material states and where a fact hangs, and inadmissible for how long a record should be or how many records a statement becomes. The bodies measured are an unruled subagent's compressions, partly overwritten by hand with nothing saying which body is which, and no write rules existed when the extraction ran. **A length bound is issued down from affordability, not read up from samples.**

| survives | does not survive |
|---|---|
| which obligations exist, and their `due`, `grade_share`, `optional` | every character count - 871 / 1,010 / 459 / 1,881 |
| which policies the course states | how many notes those policies became, and how long each is |
| that 4 notes hang on the course and 7 on obligations - a placement fact | `parts`' wording and length on every row |

## Considered Options

Keeping the numbers. Rejected as circular: it canonises an unruled agent's output as the target. Hard to reverse, because re-adopting the corpus as a shape argument means re-running the extraction under write rules. Surprising, because the corpus is the project's only real data and three record passages already lean on it.

## Consequences

This is the general form of the caveat `0037` states about the graveyard's own arithmetic: both say a body of evidence is self-disqualified, for different reasons and against different alternatives. Any deferred question about a length bound cites this ruling rather than restating it - the bound is a decision deliberately not made, and its former evidence base is void.
