# A derived record may narrow or drop its source's ruling; provenance is not authority

A document derived from another may legitimately narrow, drop or supersede what its source ruled, and a divergence between them is not by itself evidence that the derived one is in violation. The failure this prevents already has a name - *the authorising document has become history but is still cited as authority* - and treating an upstream ruling as binding downstream is that failure committed in reverse.

## Considered Options

Treating an upstream ruling as binding downstream. Rejected, and the cost of taking it is measured: a sitting that did re-sorted 7 verified *"the spec states the opposite of a ruling"* findings down to 5, of which only 1 was actually input-versus-output.

## Consequences

The rule needs the successor to carry more than one layer of document, and it does: `CONTEXT.md` above `docs/adr/` above the code. The stronger rule this repository works under - never cite a fall26 document as a reason to do something - is the ancestor-specific case of this one, not a different rule.
